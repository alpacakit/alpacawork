# Deciding the direction

Read this before building when a decision is heavy, when the user judges the direction themselves, or when the operation cannot be undone. Terms and the weight of a decision are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

- **Question the direction once, for a heavy decision.** Have one alternative with a genuinely different skeleton built rough by a new agent given only the frame and the facts — not the current version, the requested approach or your idea, because builders who start from those end up near them (build it yourself only if you have not seen the current version). Add a second only when the first ends up close to the direction. When it cannot be made in minutes at full form, make it in the roughest form that still allows the same check (an outline, a static mock, type definitions and signatures).
- **Compare them in round 1, by a rule written before the round**, on the purpose, on the test of item 1 in [frame.md](frame.md) when the audience is a person, and on problems that come from the skeleton (those survive polishing), not on what a wording fix would remove. A result with checks of whether it lands is compared by audience stand-ins ([showing.md](showing.md)); any other result by the reviewers of the Heavy row, who choose among the options and review the frame in the same request — the builder does not choose alone. If the reviewers choose differently, follow the reasons that bear on the purpose and record the split. Decide to continue or to restart from an alternative or a mix, and record why.
- **Limits on switching.** If the better alternative goes beyond what was asked, take it to the requester. When the requester has explicitly fixed the direction, keep it, report what the alternative showed, and leave any switch to the requester. When the result is one people already use (an existing screen, a command in daily use), keep its skeleton unless the comparison shows a problem in it, and take a switch that would change what users are used to to the requester.
- **When the user judges the direction themselves** (they asked for a trial, a sample or options to judge from, or the project policy reserves the choice), show them a rough version first, and again after an answer that changes the direction. When the direction is questioned (above), show the alternative beside it, as rough; their answer takes the place of choosing among the options in round 1, and the reviewers of the Heavy row review the frame in the first round after it. Rough leaves the direction open, not the version unchecked. Before they see it, check it yourself: against their words (each thing asked is in, or named as not yet in); where and as they will meet it (the main path to its end, a second visit included); and on what the frame has the builder judge (the test of item 1 in [frame.md](frame.md), the facts it may claim). Tell them what is not built or checked yet. Rounds of stand-ins and reviewers, and wide tests, wait for the direction they settle; before it, at most one round, and only for what the choice turns on and they cannot judge by looking. If a later round changes what they saw, report the change; dropping something they asked for or chose is their decision.
- **What cannot be undone** (production data, a migration, a release, sending, paying, deleting) is tried only on a copy or test data before it is run for real. For a heavy one, the reviewers of the Heavy row pass the steps of the operation (how it is tried on the copy and what must be confirmed there, written in the request that asks for the review) first; otherwise the builder checks it on the copy.
