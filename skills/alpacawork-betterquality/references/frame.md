# Framing the result (before building)

Read this before building anything that is not light work, and again when the requester asks to add to or change an existing result. Terms and the weight of a decision are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

Work with only light decisions needs no frame ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), "Weight of a decision"). Otherwise write a short frame, each item as long as the weight of its decisions needs, and only the points this work could get wrong. Write it from who the result is for and the requester's aim, not by describing the current version: a takeaway or a limit that only restates what the current version does is not yet a reason. When the requester asks to add to or change an existing result, read this skill again and decide every item anew for the result as it will stand, replacing the earlier frame, not appending to it; fix or delete the existing parts the change affects or that fail the test of item 1, and leave the rest. Each item is decided from the one before it.

1. **Who it is for, and their background.** Who uses, reads or decides with the result — a person, or another program or agent — and whatever about them shapes how they will take it: what they know and do not know, what they compare it with, in what situation they meet it and what they want at that moment, in what unit they decide or act, and where the information they use at that moment comes from. Work this out from this result; there is no fixed checklist. Facts the builder or the mechanism needs in order to work correctly are not the information a user needs at that moment; do not treat the two the same. When the audience is mixed, name each segment.
   - **A person or an AI.** Say which, because "good" differs. A person holds few things at once, skims, finds the way by headings and order, and stops when it costs too much: for each part, ask whether they could act as the purpose needs just as well without it; if so, remove it. Ask it of the result itself, not of a stand-in, which reads everything like any AI. An AI or a program reads everything and follows every reference: good is complete, exact and traceable. When both read the result, keep the person's path short and put the rest where it is looked up.
2. **Purpose.** In one sentence (one per segment when they differ), what the result must do for them. Take it from the requester's words or from what they already chose, not from how the result works. Separate the purpose from the means, and start from the smallest result that does what the words ask; anything beyond them needs a reason from the purpose. The parts of the frame not in the requester's words are your reading: confirm only the readings that would change the purpose or a check if wrong, record the rest as assumptions, and build on your reading while you wait. If the answer differs, restart from the frame; if none comes before you finish, report the reading as unconfirmed. A frame handed to you by another agent is checked the same way.
3. **What they should come away with** (when people read or use the result): in their words, leading with the purpose. Test each by asking "so that what?" and stop at what they want at that moment. Add understanding only as far as the purpose or a decision needs it. When they already know something that serves a similar purpose, explain the result by how it differs from that, not from nothing. For something they have not used, say first when or where it acts, in terms they already know ("when the AI asks you to decide"), then what it changes. Name only what they have already met on their path, not a label or a setting they have not seen yet. Put each takeaway where they can act on it: a risk they can do nothing about at that point reads as a warning and makes them hesitate, so give it where they decide about it, together with what they can do.
4. **Facts and inputs.** The sources of truth the result may claim; fixes stay within them. Hold the user's original words and the evaluations and lessons in memory against the frame. If this work could fail for the same reason as a past failed example, write that example in without rewording it — reading memory yourself does not get it to the builder or the reviewers.
5. **Form limits.** Length, one screen, compatibility, and the requester's tone (do not add patterns their style does not use). For a person, ask the test of item 1 of everything they meet on their path — what this work makes, what was already there, and what is shown with either — and remove what fails it; raise what you may not change to the requester. Each round of fixes tends to add: fresh AIs report what is missing far more readily than what is too much. When the reader has to find something in the real thing, or use something exactly as given (text they type or paste, a value they enter, what they press), show it as it actually looks in their language, with only as much around it as they need to locate it. Put what they use outside the sentences that explain it, on its own and complete, so they can take it in one move without picking it out of the text or editing it.
6. **Checks.** "Is it what was asked, and is it correct?" is always a check. When the result must work for its audience with no one there to explain, add checks of whether it lands: every takeaway is judged by at least one, and at least one judges the purpose itself as achieved for them (they fix the error on their own, start without asking, finish the task, want to try it) — not whether they can say what the result does, unless that understanding is the purpose. Judge them per segment. For a person, one check is always the test of item 1, judged by the builder on the result itself, not from stand-ins' answers. Keep 2 to 5 checks in all, each checkable from what can be observed; derive them from the frame, not from a menu.
7. **Gate.** Separate required pass conditions from optional improvements, following [the done conditions of an alpacawork request text](../../alpacawork/references/request.md), and write who finds a bad state, with what, under which conditions — the actual conditions of use, named as the paths, sizes, layouts and states the result must hold in (sizes and layouts such as a narrow width, full-screen and little room around the part; states such as what is already there, open or half done when the person arrives). A fault outside them is required only when it cannot be recovered from. For checks of whether it lands, the gate is: every check passes for every stand-in of the round, nothing blocked them (a place where they stopped, took a wrong action or could not reach the purpose), plus any line the frame needs (from the first screen only, within the form limits). Write the gate before showing the first version. You may make it stricter; loosening it is the requester's decision. If a check turns out to miss a takeaway or the purpose was wrong, fix the frame and the checks, say so in the record, and do not compare later rounds with earlier ones.

Write the frame in one place, where the builder and the reviewers read it: in the request text when another agent builds, otherwise in one file in the task folder. Keep it out of reach of audience stand-ins.
