# Rounds: showing, judging, fixing, stopping

Read this before the first showing to a fresh AI, and each time findings come back. Terms, the weight of a decision and the round cap are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

## Show it to a fresh AI

Every round goes to an AI in a conversation separate from the building; switching roles inside the same conversation does not make it fresh. Give it only what the table below lists, never the builder's explanation of what each part is meant to do. The checks decide which kind looks:

| | Audience stand-in (whether it lands) | Reviewer (as asked and correct) |
|---|---|---|
| Is given | A role, the result as the audience meets it, their task in their words, and questions — nothing about the intent | The user's words, the frame, the real thing at a version, and what the builder verified |
| Who judges | The builder, from the answers and actions | The reviewer: Pass, Fix or Rebuild |
| Next round | A new stand-in each round (being new is the point) | The same reviewer, in the same conversation, looking at what changed |
| How | [showing.md](showing.md) | [reviewer.md](reviewer.md), asked as in [alpacawork.md](alpacawork.md) |

- **When a result has both kinds of checks**: audience stand-ins until their gate is met. Then send the final check ("Stop" below) and the reviewer on that same version, in the same wait cycle. If the final check fails, the reviewer looks at what the fixes changed, together with the new final check. If the reviewer's required fixes change anything the audience sees, show the changed version to one new stand-in within the same round.
- When a round has two or more reviewers, send their requests in the same wait cycle. Bring in a new reviewer only when the skeleton was rebuilt or a viewpoint was missing.

## Judge, fix, repeat, stop

1. **Judge.** For audience stand-ins, judge each check pass or fail against the answers and the facts, and record anything that blocked them; there is no partial pass — when unsure, fail it and write why. For reviewers, sort each finding into fix, reject with grounds, or unresolved, by its effect under the conditions of use the gate names, not by the label it arrived with: check unclear grounds instead of making the result worse for mere preference; do not reject or demote a finding about a required condition without grounds; an optional improvement, a recoverable fault outside those conditions included, may be put off with the reason and the effect recorded. A finding that the gate leaves out a condition of use the user's words or the audience's situation imply is not sorted by the gate: fix the frame ([frame.md](frame.md)) or record why not.
2. **Fix the cause.** Fix the common cause once — where the stand-in stopped or what the finding names, and the other places where the same cause can occur — instead of a remedy per finding. Findings mostly name what is missing, so first try removing or merging (a repeated explanation, a rare exception, what the screen already shows); supporting work (documents, procedures, rules) counts as an addition. After a fix, ask the test of item 1 ([frame.md](frame.md)) again of everything on the reader's path, not only of what the fix added; what serves only some readers goes where it is looked up. A proposed remedy is a hint, not an instruction. A stumble that does not block and touches no check may stay, with a one-line reason; "I can't tell what this would do for me" cannot stay — the frame missed it, so go back to the frame ([frame.md](frame.md)). Do not settle a problem with the skeleton, or a recurrence, by patching; see "Stuck" below.
3. **Repeat.** Make all the fixes of a round, walk yourself every condition of use the gate names that the fixes could reach, and the ones they add, then show again. A fix made only for optional findings is checked by the builder and does not open another round. In each round run the checks the change affects; run a wide and slow check once, on the version to be delivered, after the last round that can change it.
4. **Count.** Round 1 is the comparison of skeletons (with the frame review in the same request) when there is one, otherwise the first showing. What is made on the way (the frame, a draft of a question or report, test helpers) shares the count of the result. Do not reset the count for a fix to the same result, a return to the skeleton, or a change of agent, conversation or task.
5. **Stop when one of these holds:**
   - **Gate met.** For checks of whether it lands, run one final check with a stand-in of the same segment on a different model (stand-ins of one model share habits, and the result drifts toward pleasing them); if no other model is available, the same segment written as a different person. The first final check does not count toward the cap; if it fails, fixing counts as a round and a new final check follows, unless the cap is reached. For reviewers, a Pass ends it; do not ask for another pass to make sure. If the required conditions are met, every adopted fix is made and nothing is unverified, end even at round 1. Hand over the version that passed; if you change it afterwards, check the change yourself and say it was not shown again.
   - **Cap reached.** Report the unresolved and unverified items, their effect on use, and the next action. Do not treat places fixed after the last round as re-checked; if a required item is unresolved or unverified, return the result as failed or unverified. The cap is neither a reason to pass the result nor to extend silently, and the gate is not loosened to pass it.
   - **Stuck.** Judge by failed fixes and the lack of new evidence, not by rounds or time alone: two rounds in a row in which no failing check turned to pass and no blocking problem went away; a fix for the same shortfall failed twice; fixes keep producing new problems of the same kind, even while the count of findings falls; the builder keeps returning to a dropped assumption; the estimate has doubled while the required findings do not shrink; the direction turns out to differ substantially from what was asked; or a reviewer's verdict is Rebuild (set it aside only when the reviewer got a fact or the request wrong — work already spent is not grounds). Then stop patching. When what keeps failing is one rule inside a skeleton that holds (no reviewer's Rebuild verdict stands, and every failure traces to that rule), keep the skeleton: write every case the rule must hold in, have a reviewer complete the list in one reply, sort the cases by the gate, and build that rule once against the list. Otherwise choose the skeleton again ([direction.md](direction.md)): for a small result (a few lines of text, a single label) you may do that yourself once within the cap; otherwise have a new agent, one that starts without the building conversation, do the building ([alpacawork.md](alpacawork.md)), changing the model too when the same one would likely arrive at the same skeleton. The round count carries over; if no round remains, report as at the cap, with the rule built against the list, or the rebuild, as the next action. If it stalls again, stop and report.
